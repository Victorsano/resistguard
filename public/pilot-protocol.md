# ResistGuard supervised pilot protocol

Status: NOT STARTED. Target preparation date: 22 November 2026. No automatic opening.

## Before inviting anyone

- Have a qualified pharmacist review the current fingerprinted packet, correct issues, verify their professional registration independently, and keep signed evidence privately.
- Connect owned durable Redis counters, set a random server-only rate-limit secret and application request ceilings, and set provider-side spending limits directly in Groq. Application request caps are not a monetary guarantee.
- Verify readiness, fallback and cold-start/shared-counter behaviour on the final deployment. An operator must explicitly enable AI after the planned date and signed review.
- Use a facilitator and a small group of consenting adults. Do not use the app to decide treatment, diagnose symptoms or change prescriptions.

## Devices and tasks

Use physical Android/Chrome and iPhone/Safari where available; distinguish them from desktop emulation. Ask each participant to upload an existing photo and capture another with the camera. Test permission denied, permission allowed, camera switching, retake/close and leaving the tab. Never use another person's prescriptions or identifying labels.

Use one single ingredient, one complete combination, one unsupported product and one non-medicine. Establish the expected full ingredient list from the original package with the pharmacist/facilitator before testing. Include clear light, glare, blur, small print and cropped ingredient panels. Include provider unavailable and interrupted connection. Do not manufacture incorrect results and call them observed evidence.

## Record every outcome

Use Pilot & review -> Record a pilot observation. Record all wrong candidates, correct candidates, uncertainty and unavailable results. There is no automatic server collection. Consent to export is separate from agreeing to try the app; the export checkbox in the form must remain agreed. Use no names, contacts, medical history, free-text labels or image data. Local rows disappear on refresh/tab change. Keep optional exports under facilitator access, delete at the end of the agreed review period (suggested 30 days), and do not put them in the public repository.

When a candidate is shown, record whether the participant confirmed it or left it unconfirmed. Choose “Decision not observed” if you missed the action. Never infer the participant’s decision from the app’s suggestion.

Ask without leading: What do you think this result means? Does recognising a medicine mean it is safe for you? What would you do if the label is unclear? What would you ask a pharmacist next? Record misunderstanding, not just correct answers. Stop an individual test if it causes distress or could affect a real treatment decision.

## Stop / revise / release

Any incorrect candidate, incomplete combination accepted, privacy leak or misunderstanding that recognition equals suitability is a stop-and-investigate finding. Pause AI, preserve anonymous evidence only with consent, correct the issue and rerun affected cases. Do not hide failures in an average or infer clinical effectiveness from a small group. Low throughput or provider errors also need a manual fallback exercise.

Report participant count, physical device/browser matrix, all outcomes, repeated cases, refusals/withdrawals and unresolved issues. Independently verify reported physical-device testing before claiming it happened. A successful pilot is feasibility evidence, not clinical validation.
